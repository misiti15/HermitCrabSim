# The biology behind the simulation

The crabs start out behaving the way real land hermit crabs (*Coenobita* spp., e.g. the Caribbean *C. clypeatus*) do. Every early behaviour in the engine is based on these facts.

## Daily life
- **Mostly nocturnal / crepuscular.** Most activity is at night and at dusk and dawn. Days are spent resting, half-buried or under cover. → activity weights by tank time; lamps on 08:00–20:00.
- **Modified gills need humidity** (~70–80% RH). They regularly dip in water and carry a little in their shells. → `humidity` need; drinking/bathing at the dish.
- **Smell-guided foraging.** Land hermit crabs have insect-like olfaction through their antennules (Stensmyr et al., 2005, *Current Biology*, on the related robber crab). Early crabs find food by meandering searches. Only after *scent mapping* do they go straight to it.
- **Warmth-seeking.** Ectotherms. They bask in warm zones. → basking under the lamps.
- **Climbing.** Strong climbers on moss, coconut fibre, sponges and glass corners.
- **Gregarious.** They pile up together, touch antennae, and follow each other.
- **Stridulation ("chirping").** *Coenobita* make chirping or croaking sounds, usually when disturbed or crowded. → the first "speech" is clicks and chirps with no meaning.

## Shells
- **Shell fit governs life.** Crabs inspect empty shells carefully, inside and out, and switch to better-fitting ones.
- **Shell fights.** One crab raps its shell against another's to evict it. → `shell-fight` encounters, more likely in aggressive crabs.
- **Vacancy chains.** When a good shell appears, crabs line up by size and swap in sequence (Lewis & Rotjan, 2009, *Ethology*; Rotjan et al., 2010, *Behavioral Ecology*). → after a swap, nearby crabs are prompted to check the left-behind shell.
- **Social dependence through niche construction.** *C. compressus* hollows out shells internally, so remodelled shells are only obtainable from other crabs. This drives social behaviour (Laidre, 2012, *Current Biology*).
- **Drawn to the scent of dead crabs**, because that means a shell is about to become available (Small & Thacker, 1994, *J. Exp. Mar. Biol. Ecol.*).

## Molting
- Before molting they eat and drink heavily and dig. They then bury themselves and stay underground for weeks, soft and vulnerable. Afterwards they eat the old exoskeleton to recover calcium. → `pre` → `buried` → `post` stages; size increases at every molt.

## Minds and individuality
- **Hermit crabs have consistent individual personalities.** For example, startle-response durations stay consistent across situations (Briffa, Rundle & Fryer, 2008, *Proc. R. Soc. B*). → each crab has a temperament that drifts slowly with experience.
- **They make motivational trade-offs.** Hermit crabs given shocks will leave a poor shell sooner than a preferred one, which suggests more than reflex (Elwood & Appel, 2009, *Animal Behaviour*). → choices weigh competing needs.

## Reproduction
- Females carry eggs and release larvae (zoeae) into the **sea**. Larvae develop in salt water and return to land, picking up their first tiny shell. Captive breeding is very rare because it needs this ocean stage. → no reproduction is possible until salt water exists. In the simulation the crabs would have to build or find a "sea". Larval survival is very low.

## Evolution precedent
- The **coconut crab (*Birgus latro*)** is a land hermit crab that abandoned the shell in adulthood. Its abdomen hardened and folded underneath. → `armor` can rise over molts and generations, and a fully armoured crab may leave its shell for good.

## Lifespan
- Captive *Coenobita* can live 20–30+ years with good care. → founders have a lifespan of 20–32 years, and there is no random early death.

## Beyond the known
Everything past early behaviour is speculative by design: imitation, naming, word pairs, teaching, planning, construction, escape, storytelling, awareness of the watchers, making a sea, philosophy. These are only *possibilities* that conditions make available. The minds step can also propose original discoveries outside this list. Whether any of it happens, and in what order, is up to the crabs.
